Before the Breach

Every year, tens of thousands of software security holes are made public. Attackers use only a handful. Can we spot that handful before it happens?

Scroll ↓

In 2026, 72,670 new security holes were published.

Each dot is one. Security people call them CVEs: a public ID for one flaw in one piece of software.

Only about 1 in 855 ever gets used by real attackers.

That's the rate we saw in 2024 and 2025. If 2026 is the same, about 85 of these dots will catch fire.

Defenders can't fix them all. The challenge: pick 500.

Patch the right 500 and most attacks have nowhere to land. Pick the wrong ones and the fires start anyway.

Today, teams pick with a simple rule: “Is there public attack code? Then the highest risk score.”

We tested that rule on 2024 and 2025, where we already know which holes were attacked.

It caught 25% of the attacked holes. It missed the rest.

19 caught, 56 missed, out of 75 that attackers used.

Here are those 75 attacked holes.

31 had public attack code, so the simple rule could at least see them.

44 were blind spots: no attack code and a low risk score.

The simple rule caught 0 of them. It can't. Nothing it looks at says “danger”.

Our model caught 2 of the 44.

That's small, and we won't pretend it isn't. But zero to two is the first sign of a signal the usual tools don't have: the words.

Blind-spot breakdown measured on our v4 model; the final v5 catches the same totals (12 + 9).

What IBM Granite reads

One of the two: a FortiOS flaw from 2025. No public attack code. A low risk score. Granite reads the description…

…and recognises it.

It sounds more like a known attack than 98% of 2025's holes. Its closest past attack: a Fortinet login bypass, exploited in 2022.

But 2026 is different.

Each square is one month: the typical risk score (EPSS) of new holes from each reporter. Brighter means a higher score.

From July 2026, scores jumped about 6×.

That's 62% of the 2026 list. A cut-off tuned on past years would break here, so our model compares each hole only with others from the same month.

Some data looks magical. It's too good to be true.

Our honest model caught 21. Add a few columns from today's database and watch…

100% of attacked holes are marked “Analyzed” today, against 46% of the rest.

The database reviews attacked holes first, so that field leaks the answer. On the day you'd have to choose, it wasn't filled in yet. We never use it.

The honest result

Tested on the past two years, we caught 21 attacked holes, against 19 for the best simple rule. That's a small edge, and the uncertainty range says it could be luck.

What we are sure of: the blind spots are real, 2026 shifted mid-year, and we didn't cheat.
