Your data sits behind thousands of locks. A few are already being picked.

Your bank, your doctor and every app on your phone run software with security holes. Almost all of those holes are never used. The few that are leak passwords, savings and health records. Fortres tells defenders which ones to fix first.

See the 500 to fix firstWhy it matters

When one gets used, people pay for it. Usually people who never heard of the software.

Your information

Names, card numbers, passwords and medical records are what attackers take, and they get resold or used to open accounts in your name. Think of your mom getting a call from "her bank".

Their money

A single data breach costs a company $4.99 million on average, according to IBM's Cost of a Data Breach Report 2026. That bill lands on customers too.

Everyone's time

Security teams cannot fix every hole at once. They have to choose what to fix this week, and a wrong choice leaves the door open while the team works on something harmless.

Too many holes, too few hands. So the order matters.

The warning signs everyone watches miss most of the holes that get used. So Fortres also reads what each hole actually does, and learns which kinds attackers come back to.

Read, rank, check. Three steps, all on open IBM models.

  1. 1

    Read every description

    IBM Granite, an open AI model we run on our own laptops, reads each hole's description and finds the most similar holes that attackers used before. Simple word flags tag what kind of flaw it is.

  2. 2

    Rank all 72,670

    A model trained on 2024 and 2025 scores every 2026 hole, using only what was known at the time. Each hole is compared with others from the same month, because risk scores jumped in July 2026.

  3. 3

    Explain and check

    Each pick gets a plain reason. IBM Granite Guardian then checks that the reason only says what the facts say. Reasons that fail fall back to a plain template.

Questions judges usually ask.

Is 27 against 19 just luck?
Partly, maybe. With 75 holes to test on, the 90% range for our lead runs from 2 to 14 more. Even the low end is ahead, but it is a small test and we treat it as one.
How do you know you didn't cheat?
We tested on whole years the model never saw, using only facts known at the time. We also ran a cheating test: fields filled in after an attack lift the score from 27 to 33, so we kept them out.
Where is IBM technology used?
IBM Granite reads every description and finds similar past attacks. IBM Granite Guardian checks each written reason against its facts. Both are open IBM models we ran on our own laptops. The page borrows IBM Carbon for its system parts.
What is EPSS?
A free daily score from FIRST.org that estimates how likely a hole is to be attacked in the next 30 days. Many teams decide what to fix by it.
Why 500?
It is the list size IBM's challenge asked for, and roughly what a security team can act on in a cycle.
What does Granite Guardian check?
That a reason sticks to the facts it was given. Passing that groundedness check does not mean the reason is true.
