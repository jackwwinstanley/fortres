What is fortres?

Fortres is a triage system for security holes: a statistical model ranks all 72,670 of 2026's new vulnerabilities and picks the 500 most likely to be attacked, and IBM Granite models help read the descriptions and explain every pick in plain language.

  1. The ranking is 20 models that vote, each built from 150 small decision trees. It is not a chatbot. It's fast, it can be tested against what really happened, and it runs on a laptop.
  2. IBM Granite does the reading and the explaining. Granite Embedding reads each description and gives the ranking one clue. Granite 3.3 writes a one-sentence reason for each pick, and Granite Guardian checks that reason against the facts. If it fails, a plain template sentence is used instead. Then IBM watsonx.governance on IBM Cloud scored all 500 reasons: 0 were flagged for harm or personal data.
  3. What it is not. It doesn't predict who gets attacked. The language model never decides the ranking. And it still misses most holes that give no warning.

What we found

Results come from our 2024 and 2025 test: we trained on one year and ranked the other, then checked which holes CISA later added to its list.

  3. 27not 33

    Data that arrives after an attack makes results look better than they are.

    NVD reviews holes on CISA's list first: every one of them is marked "Analyzed", against 42 to 50% of the rest. Using that review status would raise 27 catches to 33, so we left it out.

  4. 249of 490

    Outside data can carry hindsight dates.

    In Metasploit, 249 of 490 links to a hole were added more than 30 days after the attack module itself. Some 2025 holes are new numbers for exploits from 2010. VulnCheck's list dates match the first attack report in 5,803 of 5,809 entries, which looks like backfilling. We rejected VulnCheck for this, dated Metasploit by when the link was added, and adopted neither.

    outputs/newdata_provenance.md · outputs/outside_report.md
  5. 7 + 11tried, one kept

    Simpler beat clever.

    We declared 7 model variants and 11 extra methods before seeing results. The winner was the more constrained model, and no extra method passed our pre-set rule. That restraint is also why rare signals, like Nuclei and Metasploit flags, add nothing.

    outputs/rank_summary.csv · outputs/zoo_summary.md · outputs/newdata_report.md
  6. 19 → 10

    Attackers return to the same vendors.

    Hiding whole vendors from training halves the 2025 test's catches, from 19 to 10. A vendor's track record is a strong clue, and that's also a limit: a brand-new vendor gets less help.

    outputs/rank_vendor_cv.csv
  7. 67events behind 75

    Attacks come in bursts.

    The 75 holes come from 67 separate events, where one vendor's holes are added to CISA's list on the same day. Because they're linked, our ranges resample whole events, which makes them wider and more honest.

    The AI's reasons needed a referee.

    Granite Guardian rejected 90 reasons, and 92 more failed our own checks. The most common failure was a word that isn't in the facts. All 182 got a plain template sentence instead.

    Fortres's list of 441 holds all 27 catches (its 27th comes at position 353). To reach 27, you'd have to go 1,116 deep with the usual rule, 6,858 deep sorting by severity, and 10,026 deep with EPSS alone.

Questions judges ask

Is fortres an LLM?
No. The ranking is 20 models that vote, each built from 150 small decision trees. IBM Granite reads the descriptions to give the ranking one clue, and writes the plain reason for each pick, which Granite Guardian checks against the facts.
Why not use an LLM to rank?
A ranking model can be tested against known outcomes, and it gives the same answer every time. A language model can make things up, so we only let it explain.
