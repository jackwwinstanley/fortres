# Page content (DRAFT, content only, no design)

What each section of the public page should *say* and which real numbers it shows. Written for very non-technical
judges: one plain sentence first, numbers second. Every number names the file it comes from, and all of them are
regenerated by `python -m src.b.export_story` (→ `site/data/story.json`) unless another script is named.
The visual design is deliberately undecided.

---

## 0. The haystack
**Message:** In 2026, 72,670 new security holes were published. Only about 1 in 855 ever gets used by real attackers.
- 72,670 CVEs on the 2026 scoreboard (`outputs/scoreboard_features.parquet`)
- 75 of 64,119 practice-year CVEs were attacked, so 1 in 855; at that rate about 85 of 2026's holes would be
  (`outputs/replica_2024.parquet`, `replica_2025.parquet`, `label`)
- Defenders can patch 500 (the challenge rule).

## 1. Why attackers pick some holes
**Message:** Out of every 1,000 holes, about 6 get attacked. Some kinds get attacked about 3× as often, and some
scary-sounding kinds almost never.

| kind of hole | attacked per 1,000 | attacked / holes |
|---|---|---|
| all holes (2018–Sep 2025) | 5.6 | 1,131 / 201,494 |
| enterprise software | 25.5 | 295 / 11,555 |
| public attack code exists (2024–25 practice years) | 22.3 | 31 / 1,393 |
| web browser | 18.3 | 97 / 5,308 |
| network edge device (firewall, VPN) | 17.3 | 87 / 5,016 |
| takes over the machine | 15.9 | 404 / 25,429 |
| no login needed | 15.7 | 247 / 15,765 |
| **cross-site scripting** | **0.7** | **22 / 31,018** |
| **WordPress plugin** | **0.2** | **3 / 13,777** |

**The surprise:** WordPress plugins and cross-site scripting make up a big share of all published holes, but attackers
almost never use them. Scary-sounding isn't the same as attacked.
Source: `site/data/why_traits.csv`. "Added to KEV" means on CISA's KEV list as of 2025-12-29 (wording: "added to CISA KEV", "associated with", never "causes"). Traits are word tags on the
description. Descriptive only: never a model input. Footnote: older holes had more time to be attacked.

## 2. Today's rule and its blind spots
**Message:** The usual rule ("public attack code first, then the highest risk score") caught 19 of the 75 attacked
holes in our practice years: 25%. 44 of the 75 had no attack code and a low risk score, and the rule caught none of them.
- Best simple rule: 6 of 27 (2024), 13 of 48 (2025) → 19 of 75 (`outputs/runlog.csv`, `baseline_exploit_epss`)
- 31 visible (had public attack code) vs 44 blind spots (`outputs/blindspot_positives.csv`, Person A's definition)
- Simple rule on blind spots: 0. Our v5 model: 2, both Fortinet FortiOS flaws (CVE-2025-24472, CVE-2024-23113)
  (`outputs/v5_blindspots.csv`, from `python -m src.b.leakage`). **Say it honestly: small.**

## 3. What IBM Granite reads
**Message:** Granite reads the description the way an analyst would and recognises "this sounds like a past attack."
- Example: CVE-2025-24472 (FortiOS, 2025): no public attack code, low risk score, so a blind spot. Granite's score
  puts it above 98% of 2025's holes. Its closest past attack is CVE-2022-40684, the Fortinet authentication bypass
  added to KEV in 2022 (text similarity 0.90). Our model caught it. (`story.json → example`, `outputs/text_features.parquet`)
- Phrases that light up: "authentication bypass", "remote unauthenticated attacker", "super-admin privileges", "FortiOS".
- Leak guard: phrases that only appear after an attack ("exploited in the wild", ...) are deleted before Granite reads
  anything. Removing them changed accuracy by less than 0.003 (`outputs/b_text_report.md`).

## 4. 2026 changed in July
**Message:** January–May 2026 looked like past years. From July 2026, the risk scores of new holes jumped about 6×, and
those months are 62% of the 2026 list. A rule tuned on past years would break, so our model compares each hole only
with others from the same month.
- Median EPSS: 0.043% (2024–25), 0.035% (Jan–May 2026), 0.274% (Jul–Sep 2026); Jul–Sep = 61.7% of the scoreboard
  (`story.json → heat`, `docs/figures/drift_epss.png`)
- A model can tell 2025 holes from 2026 holes with AUC 0.93 (`outputs/b_drift.json`)
- No June 2026 snapshot exists in the organisers' data; June's holes are scored in July.

## 5. Too good to be true (the leakage test)
**Message:** Some data in today's database leaks the answer. Our model would look like it catches 28 instead of 21 if
we used it, so we don't.
- Honest v5 bag 21 (12 + 9) vs with after-the-fact columns 28 (17 + 11) (`outputs/leakage_results.csv`; on v4 it was 29)
- 100% of attacked holes are marked "Analyzed" in today's NVD, vs 46% of the others (`story.json → leak`)
- No leak in the honest model: independent column review (`docs/column_review.md`) and Person A's audit (`outputs/v5_report.md`)

## 6. Our 500 for 2026, in plain groups

## 7. The honest result
**Message:** Tested on the past two years, we caught 21 attacked holes against 19 for the best simple rule. That's a
small edge, and the uncertainty range says it could be luck. What we're sure of: the blind spots are real, 2026 shifted
in July, and we didn't cheat.
- v5 20-seed bag: 12 (2025) + 9 (2024) = 21 vs 13 + 6 = 19; pooled difference +2, 90% range −4 to +8
  (`outputs/headline.json`)
- Forward-in-time test alone (train 2024 → test 2025): 12 vs 13, range −6 to +3. **Don't hide this**; it's the strictest test.
